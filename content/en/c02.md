## 🏢 The situation

Dasha, the CMO, comes over with a complaint about the research system the team assembled a month ago:

> "I asked it: 'what does competitor X charge for a subscription'. It thought for six minutes and sent me a four-page report where the answer is in the second paragraph. Then I asked 'what is actually happening in the agentic assistant market in our region' — it took a minute and gave me three links. That's it."

You look at the architecture. Four subagents: web search, document analysis, synthesis, report generation. And one coordinator, written like this:

```python
def research(query):
    sources  = web_agent(query)
    parsed   = docs_agent(sources)
    findings = synth_agent(parsed)
    return report_agent(findings)
```

Both symptoms come from one line of that code — or rather, from what is missing in it. The pipeline is always the same: it never looks at the query. A simple fact travels through four agents and costs as much as a full research project. A broad topic gets decomposed into the same narrow subqueries as a pointed one, so coverage ends up accidental.

There is a second problem you find in the logs: `synth_agent` occasionally mentions a company that appeared in no source at all. Digging in — on a parsing failure `docs_agent` writes `"see previous discussion"` into its answer, assuming the synthesiser saw the coordinator's conversation. It did not.

## 🎯 Your task

1. Understand how hub-and-spoke differs from "agents talking to each other", and why the exam insists on the former.
2. Explain why a subagent does not see the coordinator's context — and what that implies for how a task is written.
3. Rewrite the coordinator so it picks subagents to fit the query instead of running everyone every time.

## 📚 Theory

### Hub-and-spoke: all communication goes through the centre

In a coordinator-subagent architecture, subagents **do not talk to each other**. The coordinator is the single point through which tasks, results, errors and routing all pass.

The coordinator carries four responsibilities, and it is worth memorising them as a list:

1. **Decomposition** — break the request into subtasks.
2. **Delegation** — decide which subagents are needed and phrase each task.
3. **Aggregation** — assemble the results into one answer.
4. **Error handling and routing** — a subagent crashed, returned garbage, or timed out: the coordinator decides what happens.

### Subagent context is isolated

This is the domain's most common trap. A subagent **does not automatically inherit the coordinator's conversation history**. It sees exactly what you passed in the task — and nothing else.

```
Coordinator:  [full history: Dasha's request, the plan, web_agent's results, ...]
                    │
                    └── task → Subagent synth: [ONLY the task text]
                                                nothing about Dasha's request,
                                                nothing about what web_agent found,
                                                unless you wrote it in
```

Hence the phantom company: `docs_agent` wrote "see previous discussion" expecting shared context, `synth_agent` received a sentence with no content and filled in the gap itself. Context isolation is not a flaw but a property: it keeps each subagent in a small focus and saves tokens. But it means **the task must be self-contained**: the goal, all the data needed, and the expected format.

### Dynamic selection instead of a pipeline

The exam explicitly asks for the skill: the coordinator **analyses the query's requirements and selects whom to invoke**, rather than always running everyone.

The guide states the second failure outright: **the risk of overly narrow task decomposition is incomplete coverage of broad research topics**. A coordinator that only knows how to produce pointed subqueries will answer "what is happening in the market" with three unrelated facts and call it a survey. A broad topic needs a different cut — by dimension: players, pricing, technology, regulation — and more subagents, not fewer.

A practical rule: the coordinator first classifies the request — **fact / comparison / survey** — and only then builds a plan. That single decision removes both of Dasha's symptoms.

## 💻 Practice

A coordinator with dynamic subagent selection. Classification is a separate call with structured output, so the decision shows up in the logs and can be checked.

```python
# pip install anthropic
import os, json, anthropic

client = anthropic.Anthropic(api_key=os.environ["ANTHROPIC_API_KEY"])
MODEL = "claude-sonnet-5"

PLAN_TOOL = [{
    "name": "submit_plan",
    "description": "Record the research plan: the kind of query and which subagents are needed.",
    "input_schema": {
        "type": "object",
        "properties": {
            "kind": {"type": "string", "enum": ["fact", "comparison", "survey"],
                     "description": "fact — one verifiable fact; comparison — 2-3 objects side by side; survey — a broad overview"},
            "agents": {"type": "array", "items": {"type": "string", "enum": ["web", "docs", "synth", "report"]},
                       "description": "Whom to invoke, in execution order. A fact usually needs just ['web']."},
            "subtasks": {"type": "array", "items": {"type": "string"},
                         "description": "Self-contained tasks. For a survey, cut by dimension (players, pricing, technology) rather than three random questions."},
        },
        "required": ["kind", "agents", "subtasks"],
    },
}]

def plan(query: str) -> dict:
    resp = client.messages.create(
        model=MODEL, max_tokens=1024, tools=PLAN_TOOL,
        tool_choice={"type": "tool", "name": "submit_plan"},
        messages=[{"role": "user", "content":
            f"Build a research plan for the request: \"{query}\".\n"
            "Do not invoke subagents that are not needed for the answer: each call costs a minute and money.\n"
            "For a broad topic, do the opposite — cut it by dimension so the survey does not come out full of holes."}],
    )
    return next(b.input for b in resp.content if b.type == "tool_use")

def subagent(role: str, task: str, inputs: str) -> str:
    """A subagent does NOT see the coordinator's history: everything it needs arrives in the task."""
    system = {
        "web":    "You find facts in public sources. Answer in bullet points, each with its source.",
        "docs":   "You analyse documents. If data is missing, say so — never fill the gap yourself.",
        "synth":  "You combine findings. Use ONLY the data provided; state gaps explicitly.",
        "report": "You write the final answer for an executive: conclusion first, then the reasoning.",
    }[role]
    resp = client.messages.create(
        model=MODEL, max_tokens=1500, system=system,
        messages=[{"role": "user", "content":
            f"Task: {task}\n\nInput data (you have no other context):\n{inputs or '— none —'}"}],
    )
    return "".join(b.text for b in resp.content if b.type == "text")

def research(query: str) -> str:
    p = plan(query)
    print(f"  query kind: {p['kind']} · subagents: {p['agents']}")
    collected, errors = [], []
    for role in p["agents"]:
        task = "; ".join(p["subtasks"]) if role in ("web", "docs") else f"Original request: {query}"
        try:
            out = subagent(role, task, "\n\n".join(collected))
        except Exception as e:                      # errors converge on the coordinator too
            errors.append(f"{role}: {e}")
            continue
        collected.append(f"[{role}]\n{out}")
    if errors:
        print(f"  ⚠ subagent failures: {errors}")
    return collected[-1] if collected else "Could not assemble an answer"

if __name__ == "__main__":
    for q in ["What does competitor X charge for a subscription?",
              "What is happening in the agentic assistant market?"]:
        print(f"\n=== {q}")
        print(research(q)[:600])
```

**Assignment.** Three experiments:

1. Compare the plans for the two queries: the first should come back as `kind: "fact"` with a single subagent, the second as `survey` with a cut by dimension. If the planner invokes all four for a simple fact, sharpen the sentence about call cost in the prompt.
2. Remove the `inputs` substitution from `subagent` — and watch `synth` start citing data it never saw. That is exactly the `docs_agent` bug from the situation.
3. Make one subagent fail (`raise RuntimeError` inside the `docs` role) and confirm the coordinator records it and continues instead of taking the whole request down.

## 🎯 On the exam

- **Objective 1.2** is covered: hub-and-spoke, subagent context isolation, the coordinator's four responsibilities, dynamic selection, and the narrow-decomposition risk.
- The scenario it lives in: **Multi-Agent Research System** — nearly every coordination question routes through it. It also surfaces in **Customer Support Resolution Agent** when the agent delegates part of the work.
- Wording trap: options like "let subagents exchange results directly to avoid the coordinator bottleneck" sound like an optimisation but contradict hub-and-spoke — errors travel through the centre too.
- Second trap: "pass the coordinator's full history to the subagent." That defeats the point of isolation — the right answer is about a self-contained task, not shared context.
- Remember that narrow decomposition is a **named risk** in the official guide. If a question involves a broad topic and a complaint about incompleteness, that is almost certainly the answer.

---

## 📌 Remember

- Subagents do not talk to each other. All communication, all error handling and all routing go through the coordinator.
- A subagent does not inherit the coordinator's history. The task must be self-contained: goal, data, format.
- The coordinator's four jobs: decomposition, delegation, aggregation, error handling.
- A good coordinator decides whom to invoke based on query complexity. An always-everyone pipeline burns both money and a minute of someone's time.
- Slicing a broad topic too narrowly produces a survey full of holes. That is its own failure, and no rarer than the extra calls.
