## 🏢 The situation

Monday, 9:40. You are an AI Engineer at **Datacore** — the same company where you did the ML track: a marketplace with 2M active users and a fintech product. Except now you wear two hats: classic ML and agentic systems.

A message from **Marina, the CEO**, lands in the company chat:

> "Everyone — a competitor just launched an AI agent that runs their entire outbound marketing. I want the same **marketing factory** here, by the end of the quarter. Who owns AI for us? Meeting room at 10:00."

At 10:00 there are three people in the room, and each of them hears something different in the word "factory":

- **Dasha, CMO:** "I need ten times more content. I have two copywriters and they are burning out."
- **Igor, Head of Sales:** "I don't need content. I need emails to specific leads that I'm not embarrassed to send."
- **Victor, CFO:** "What does it cost per month, and what do we stop paying for once it works?"

Marina looks at you: "So — can we do it this quarter?"

The decision made here shapes the next three months. It is not a technical decision, it is a **framing** one. Nobody in that room said what the agent should actually do. "Marketing factory" is not a task, it is a wish.

## 🎯 Your task

1. Turn the CEO's one-liner into a concrete project: what the agent does, what it must **never** do, and how success is measured.
2. Choose an autonomy level based on the cost of a mistake, not on what the model is capable of.
3. Slice the "factory" into a first cut you can show in two weeks, not in a quarter.

## 📚 Theory

### What an "agentic system" actually is

An agent is **an LLM + tools + a loop + a goal**. The model does not just answer with text: it decides which tool to call, looks at the result and decides again — until it reaches the goal or hits a limit.

Between "just a prompt" and "an autonomous agent" there is a whole spectrum, and most working systems do not live at the right-hand edge:

| Level | What it is | Example at Datacore |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Prompt | One call, one answer | Rewrite this paragraph |
| Chain | Several calls, fixed order | Summarize → translate → format |
| Workflow | Branching and tools, route defined in code | Parse an email → if a complaint, open a ticket |
| Agent | The model picks the next step itself | Gather data on a lead and write the email |

The further right, the higher the ceiling on value — and the higher the cost of a mistake, the debugging difficulty and the token bill. **The right edge is a tool, not a goal.**

### The three questions that decide the project's fate

You ask them before the first line of code. Without answers, the project ends as a demo nobody adopts.

**1. What is the unit of work?**

Not "a factory", but something you can name in the singular: *one email to one lead*, *one triaged reply*, *one post*. Anything you cannot count one at a time cannot be estimated, measured for quality, or priced.

**2. What does "done well" mean, in numbers?**

Not "better content", but a metric a script computes without a human in the room:

| Bad metric | Why it fails | Better replacement |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "The content got better" | Not countable | Share of drafts sent without edits |
| "The agent is fast" | Not about value | Replies per 100 emails sent |
| "We saved time" | Measured by whom? | Copywriter hours per 20 emails: before/after |

**3. What does a mistake cost?**

A typo in a draft a human proofreads anyway costs nothing. An email to five thousand customers quoting a price that is not in the price list costs money, reputation and a conversation with legal.

The cost of a mistake is the only honest argument in an autonomy debate. Not "the model is smart enough now", but "if it gets this wrong, here is what we lose".

The rule is simple: **autonomy is earned with numbers**. "200 consecutive drafts, 92% sent without edits, zero invented facts" is an argument. "It feels like it writes well now" is not.

### A one-page Agent Design Doc

This is what you bring Marina instead of "sure, one quarter". It takes an hour to fill in and saves weeks.

| Section | For the marketing factory |
| --- | --- |
| Job to be done | Fill the outbound pipeline without growing the team |
| Unit of work | One personalized email to one lead from the ICP list |
| Inputs | A CRM row: company, industry, role, recent trigger event |
| Tools | Read CRM, search the company website, create an email draft |
| Outputs | Subject, body, self-check against a checklist |
| **Never do** | Quote prices, terms or discounts that are not in the price list; invent facts about the recipient; send without approval |
| Human gate | Sending happens only after a human clicks "Send" |
| Success metric | Share of drafts sent without edits; replies per 100 emails |
| Failure modes | No data on the lead → skip; model unavailable → queue |
| Budget | Cost per run × 20 emails/day × 22 days ≤ the CFO's limit |

The "Never do" section is the most important and the most often skipped. It is exactly what turns into the system prompt, the self-check list and your test cases.

## 💻 Practice

The unit of work is defined — one lead produces one draft. Now build it end to end: the "Never do" rules go into the system prompt, the answer comes back structured, and the cost is printed immediately, so you walk into Victor's office with a number rather than a feeling.

```python
# pip install anthropic
import os, json, anthropic

client = anthropic.Anthropic(api_key=os.environ["ANTHROPIC_API_KEY"])
MODEL = "claude-sonnet-5"

# Price per million tokens — put in the current numbers from your model's price list
PRICE_IN, PRICE_OUT = 3.0, 15.0

# Unit of work: one CRM row → one draft
LEAD = {
    "company": "NordFin",
    "industry": "fintech, 400 employees",
    "name": "Irina",
    "role": "Head of Data",
    "trigger": "posted an ML engineer job opening last week",
}

# The "Never do" section of the Design Doc becomes part of the prompt, verbatim
SYSTEM = """You write a first cold email on behalf of Datacore — a platform for teams
shipping ML into their product.

NEVER (a violation means the draft is rejected):
- do not quote prices, terms, discounts or guarantees;
- do not invent facts about the recipient's company — use only what is given;
- do not promise results in numbers;
- keep the body under 120 words.

REQUIRED: one specific reason why you are writing to them, and one question at the end.

Return strict JSON:
{"subject": str, "body": str,
 "self_check": {"invented_facts": bool, "mentions_price": bool, "has_question": bool, "words": int}}"""

resp = client.messages.create(
    model=MODEL,
    max_tokens=800,
    temperature=0.7,          # variety helps for drafts; for parsing inbound mail it does not
    system=SYSTEM,
    messages=[{"role": "user", "content": json.dumps(LEAD)}],
)

draft = json.loads(resp.content[0].text)
check = draft["self_check"]

# Quality gate: the agent checked itself, we decide whether a human ever sees it
ok = (not check["invented_facts"] and not check["mentions_price"]
      and check["has_question"] and check["words"] <= 120)

print("SUBJECT:", draft["subject"])
print(draft["body"])
print("\nSelf-check:", check, "→", "queued for approval" if ok else "REJECTED, rewrite")

# Economics: this is the number you bring to the CFO
cost = (resp.usage.input_tokens * PRICE_IN + resp.usage.output_tokens * PRICE_OUT) / 1_000_000
print(f"\nPer run: ${cost:.4f} · 20 emails/day × 22 days = ${cost * 20 * 22:.2f}/month")
```

What matters here, and carries over to every agent you build next:

1. **The prohibitions live in the prompt, verbatim.** Not "be polite", but checkable rules.
2. **The output is structured.** JSON instead of free text — otherwise the next stage of the pipeline can do nothing with it.
3. **The self-check is part of the output.** A cheap filter before a human sees the draft. It does not replace evals (module A6), but it catches the obvious failures.
4. **The cost is printed immediately.** "$X per month" turns the CFO conversation from an argument into arithmetic.

## 📌 Remember

- "Build us an AI agent" is not a specification. A specification starts with a unit of work you can name in the singular.
- The autonomy level follows from the cost of a mistake, not from the model's capabilities.
- A metric a script cannot compute is not a metric, it is a hope.
- The first release is one narrow lane, end to end, with a human in the loop. Autonomy is earned with statistics.
- The "Never do" section is your future system prompt and your future test cases. Skip it and you will be fixing it in production.
- A junior builds the autonomous agent first and debugs it for a month. A senior ships "draft + approval" in two weeks and brings back numbers.

**Further reading:** Anthropic, "Building effective agents" (on the workflow → agent spectrum); Teresa Torres on framing the job to be done; and any large company's internal post-mortem of an automated-email incident — the best source for understanding the cost of a mistake.
