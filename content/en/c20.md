## 🏢 The situation

The last week before the exam, and two decisions land on your desk that have to be made properly.

**Decision one.** The extraction system from C15–C16 shows **97% accuracy** on a sample. Viktor does the maths: three operators review every extraction by hand, which costs a fortune a year. "97% — let us switch off manual review at least for the confident cases."

You ask for the breakdown. It arrives:

| Document type | Share | Accuracy |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Format A invoices (large suppliers) | 71% | 99.4% |
| Format B invoices | 18% | 96.1% |
| Handwritten acceptance certificates | 8% | 71% |
| Low-quality scans | 3% | 64% |

The 97% average rests on 71% of documents of a single type. On handwritten certificates every third extraction is wrong — and those are exactly the documents whose errors go straight to accounting.

**Decision two.** Dasha presents a research report to the board in two days. It states: "The agentic assistant market is $4.2B." Where from is not said. You dig: one source gives $4.2B, another $6.8B. The synthesiser picked the first because it came earlier in the list.

It gets worse. Look at the dates: $4.2B is 2024 data, $6.8B is 2026. That is not a contradiction, it is market growth. But the report contains no dates, no second figure, and no mention that there were two sources.

## 🎯 Your task

1. Understand what an aggregate 97% hides, and what must be checked before automating.
2. Build review routing on calibrated confidence plus sampling-based control.
3. Preserve claim-source mappings through synthesis and handle the conflict correctly.

The four points on the diagram are exactly the skills of objective 5.5, and each is worth spelling out:

**A breakdown by type and field** — before automating, not after. "97%" says nothing about where the 3% lives. For Viktor it lives exactly where an error costs most.

**Field-level confidence** — not document-level. An extraction can be confident about the invoice number and unsure about the total; the reviewer needs to know about the total. Thresholds are **calibrated using labeled validation sets** — so "high" means a measured error rate, not the model's mood.

**Routing** — extractions with low model confidence **and** extractions from ambiguous or contradictory documents go to review. Reviewer capacity is limited and is spent where the error probability is highest.

**Stratified random sampling of confident extractions** — an ongoing process, not a one-off check. It measures the real error rate in the automated stream and **detects novel error patterns**: a supplier changes their template, confident extractions start lying, and the sample is how you find out.

### Attribution is lost in synthesis

The second story is objective 5.6. The loss mechanism is stated directly: **source attribution is lost during summarization steps when findings are compressed without preserving claim-source mappings**.

The remedy is not asking for citations but demanding structure: subagents output **structured claim → source mappings**, and the synthesis agent must **preserve and merge** them.

```json
{
  "claim": "market size $4.2B",
  "source_name": "Market Report 2026",
  "source_url": "https://...",
  "excerpt": "…the market reached $4.2B…",
  "published": "2024-11",
  "collected": "2024-Q3"
}
```

**Conflicting statistics.** When two credible sources give different numbers, the guide requires **annotating the conflict with source attribution rather than arbitrarily selecting one value**. Document analysis completes with **both values included and explicitly annotated**, and the coordinator decides how to reconcile them before synthesis.

**Temporal data.** Here is the key to Dasha's story: the guide requires **publication or data collection dates in structured outputs** so **temporal differences are not misinterpreted as contradictions**. $4.2B for 2024 and $6.8B for 2026 is not a conflict, it is a trend. Without dates the two are indistinguishable.

**Report structure.** Explicit sections separating **well-established findings from contested ones**, preserving the original source characterisations and methodological context. The reader must see what is solid and what is disputed.

**And the presentation form.** The objective's last skill, easily forgotten: **different content types are rendered appropriately** — financial data as tables, news as prose, technical findings as structured lists. Flattening everything to one format loses meaning along with form.

## 💻 Practice

```python
# pip install anthropic
import os, json, random, statistics, anthropic

client = anthropic.Anthropic(api_key=os.environ["ANTHROPIC_API_KEY"])
MODEL = "claude-sonnet-5"

# ---------- 5.5: field-level confidence and review routing ----------
EXTRACT_TOOL = [{
    "name": "extract_with_confidence",
    "description": "Extract invoice fields with PER-FIELD confidence.",
    "input_schema": {"type": "object", "properties": {
        "document_type": {"type": "string", "enum": ["invoice_a", "invoice_b", "handwritten", "poor_scan"]},
        "fields": {"type": "array", "items": {"type": "object", "properties": {
            "name": {"type": "string"},
            "value": {"type": ["string", "number", "null"]},
            "confidence": {"type": "number", "description": "0..1, confidence in THIS field"},
            "evidence": {"type": "string", "description": "The document fragment the value came from"},
        }, "required": ["name", "value", "confidence"]}},
        "source_ambiguous": {"type": "boolean",
                             "description": "true when the document is illegible or self-contradictory"},
    }, "required": ["document_type", "fields", "source_ambiguous"]},
}]

# Thresholds are calibrated on a labeled set — per document type, not one number for everything
CALIBRATED_THRESHOLDS = {"invoice_a": 0.90, "invoice_b": 0.93, "handwritten": 0.99, "poor_scan": 0.99}

def route_for_review(extraction: dict) -> dict:
    t = extraction["document_type"]
    threshold = CALIBRATED_THRESHOLDS[t]
    low = [f for f in extraction["fields"] if f["confidence"] < threshold]
    needs_human = bool(low) or extraction["source_ambiguous"]
    return {"auto": not needs_human, "threshold": threshold,
            "flagged_fields": [f["name"] for f in low],
            "reason": ("ambiguous_source" if extraction["source_ambiguous"]
                       else "low_confidence_fields" if low else "clean")}

def accuracy_by_segment(labeled: list[dict]) -> dict:
    """Before automating: accuracy by document type AND by field, not on average."""
    by_type, by_field = {}, {}
    for r in labeled:
        by_type.setdefault(r["type"], []).append(r["correct"])
        by_field.setdefault(r["field"], []).append(r["correct"])
    fmt = lambda d: {k: f"{100 * statistics.mean(v):.1f}% (n={len(v)})" for k, v in d.items()}
    overall = 100 * statistics.mean(r["correct"] for r in labeled)
    return {"overall": f"{overall:.1f}%", "by_type": fmt(by_type), "by_field": fmt(by_field)}

def stratified_sample(auto_approved: list[dict], per_stratum: int = 20) -> list[dict]:
    """Ongoing sampling of confident extractions: measure the real error rate and catch new patterns."""
    strata = {}
    for e in auto_approved:
        strata.setdefault(e["document_type"], []).append(e)
    return [x for group in strata.values() for x in random.sample(group, min(per_stratum, len(group)))]

# ---------- 5.6: claim → source mappings and conflict handling ----------
def merge_claims(subagent_outputs: list[list[dict]]) -> dict:
    """Synthesis PRESERVES attribution and never picks between conflicting numbers."""
    by_topic = {}
    for out in subagent_outputs:
        for c in out:
            by_topic.setdefault(c["topic"], []).append(c)

    established, contested = [], []
    for topic, claims in by_topic.items():
        values = {c["value"] for c in claims}
        if len(values) == 1:
            established.append({"topic": topic, "value": claims[0]["value"], "sources": claims})
        else:
            dates = {c.get("published") for c in claims}
            contested.append({
                "topic": topic,
                # both values stay in the report, with sources and dates
                "values": [{"value": c["value"], "source": c["source_name"],
                            "published": c.get("published"), "excerpt": c.get("excerpt")} for c in claims],
                # different dates are not necessarily a contradiction
                "likely_temporal": len(dates) > 1,
                "note": ("the values cover different periods — likely a trend rather than a conflict"
                         if len(dates) > 1 else "conflict between sources covering the same period"),
            })
    return {"established": established, "contested": contested}

def render_report(merged: dict) -> str:
    """Different content types, different forms: finance as a table, disputes in their own section."""
    lines = ["## Well-established findings\n"]
    for e in merged["established"]:
        srcs = ", ".join(f"{s['source_name']} ({s.get('published','no date')})" for s in e["sources"])
        lines.append(f"- **{e['topic']}**: {e['value']} — {srcs}")
    lines.append("\n## Contested data\n")
    lines.append("| Metric | Value | Source | Period |")
    lines.append("| --- | --- | --- | --- |")
    for c in merged["contested"]:
        for v in c["values"]:
            lines.append(f"| {c['topic']} | {v['value']} | {v['source']} | {v.get('published','—')} |")
        lines.append(f"\n> {c['note']}\n")
    return "\n".join(lines)

if __name__ == "__main__":
    labeled = ([{"type": "invoice_a", "field": "total", "correct": 1} for _ in range(140)]
             + [{"type": "invoice_a", "field": "total", "correct": 0} for _ in range(1)]
             + [{"type": "handwritten", "field": "total", "correct": 1} for _ in range(11)]
             + [{"type": "handwritten", "field": "total", "correct": 0} for _ in range(5)])
    print(json.dumps(accuracy_by_segment(labeled), indent=2))

    web = [{"topic": "market size", "value": "$4.2B", "source_name": "Market Report 2026",
            "published": "2024-11", "excerpt": "the market reached $4.2B"}]
    docs = [{"topic": "market size", "value": "$6.8B", "source_name": "Industry Outlook",
             "published": "2026-03", "excerpt": "market size of $6.8B"}]
    print("\n" + render_report(merge_claims([web, docs])))
```

**Assignment.**

1. Run `accuracy_by_segment` on your own data and find a segment more than five points below the average. Then ask yourself Viktor's question: can review be automated for it?
2. Replace the values in `CALIBRATED_THRESHOLDS` with a single 0.9 and see how many handwritten documents enter the automated stream.
3. Remove the `published` fields from the data and run `merge_claims`. Confirm the two values turn into a "conflict" although they describe market growth.

## 🎯 On the exam

- **Objective 5.5**: the masking aggregate metric, stratified random sampling of confident extractions, field-level confidence calibrated on labeled sets, verifying accuracy by type and field before automating, routing low confidence and contradictory documents to review.
- **Objective 5.6**: attribution lost in summarisation, structured claim-source mappings, annotating conflicts instead of arbitrary selection, publication dates against false contradictions, established/contested sections, appropriate rendering per content type.
- Scenarios: **Structured Data Extraction** (5.5), **Multi-Agent Research System** (5.6).
- 5.5 trap: "97% overall accuracy is enough to automate". The expected answer is a breakdown by document type and field first.
- Second 5.5 trap: "sample extractions for review at random" without stratification, or "review only the unconfident ones". You need both: confidence routing plus stratified sampling of the confident ones — otherwise novel error patterns never surface.
- 5.6 trap: "choose the more reliable source" on conflicting statistics. The guide requires annotating the conflict with attribution and leaving the decision to the coordinator.
- And remember the dates: figures from different periods differing is not a contradiction. Missing dates in structured output is itself a defect.

---

## 📌 Remember

- Aggregate accuracy masks segments. Before automating, verify accuracy per document type and per field.
- Confidence must be field-level, and thresholds calibrated on a labeled set — otherwise "high confidence" means nothing.
- Low confidence and ambiguous or contradictory documents route to review: reviewer capacity is finite.
- Stratified random sampling of confident extractions runs continuously — it is what catches novel error patterns.
- Attribution is lost in summarisation. Subagents emit structured claim-source mappings and synthesis preserves and merges them.
- Conflicting values are annotated with their sources rather than resolved by arbitrary choice.
- Publication and collection dates are mandatory: without them a temporal difference reads as a contradiction.
- A report separates established from contested findings, and the presentation form matches the content type.
