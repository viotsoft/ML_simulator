## 🏢 The situation

Quality assurance brings a review of four tickets from the past week. All four are about when the agent should have stopped and did not — or the reverse.

**Ticket 1.** Customer: "Put me through to a human." Agent: "I will try to help myself, tell me more about the problem." Four more turns of questions, and only then an escalation. The ticket scored one out of five.

**Ticket 2.** An irritated customer writes: "Your delivery again!" — while the question is simple, the order is found and a replacement is one step away. The agent saw the negativity and escalated immediately. An operator spent seven minutes on something that resolved automatically.

**Ticket 3.** A customer asks to match a competitor's price. The policy describes price adjustments on our own site only — it says nothing about competitors. The agent decided that "not forbidden" meant allowed and approved a discount.

**Ticket 4.** A name search returned three customers surnamed Petrova. The agent picked the first — by most recent order — and cancelled the wrong person's order.

And a fifth story, from the research system: the web search subagent timed out and returned `{"error": "search unavailable"}` to the coordinator. The coordinator could not tell what happened and terminated the entire investigation. Another time the same subagent returned an empty list **as a success** when it found nothing, and the report gained a section reading "no competitors found" — although the search had simply not run.

## 🎯 Your task

1. Define escalation criteria that do not depend on tone or "confidence".
2. Teach the agent to ask for clarification instead of choosing by heuristic.
3. Make subagent errors actionable for the coordinator.

The guide lists the **appropriate escalation triggers**: the customer requests a human; a policy exception or gap (not just a complex case, but a gap); an inability to make meaningful progress.

And it explains separately why **sentiment-based escalation and self-reported confidence scores are unreliable proxies** for actual case complexity. Ticket 2 is the direct illustration: the negativity is there, the complexity is not.

The exam likes the subtlety between tickets 1 and 2:

- **the customer explicitly demands a human** — honour it **immediately**, without first attempting investigation;
- **the customer is frustrated but the issue is simple** — acknowledge the frustration and offer resolution; escalate **if they reiterate** their preference.

Ticket 3 is a policy gap. The policy covers price adjustments on our own site and is **silent** about competitors. Silence is not permission: this is exactly an escalation case.

Ticket 4 is **multiple matches**. When a tool returns several customers, the agent must **ask for additional identifiers** rather than selecting on a heuristic like "most recent order". The heuristic here is a bet placed on a person who will lose their order.

All of this is implemented as **explicit escalation criteria with few-shot examples in the system prompt** — the same technique as module C14: a criterion plus demonstrated reasoning.

### An error the coordinator can act on

The fifth story is objective 5.3. `{"error": "search unavailable"}` is useless: the coordinator does not know what was attempted, what was already found, or what to do next.

**Structured error context** carries four things:

```json
{
  "failure_type": "timeout",
  "attempted": "search for 'competitor pricing', 2 attempts, 30s limit",
  "partial_results": [{"claim": "…", "source_url": "…"}],
  "alternatives": ["search each competitor separately",
                   "use last week's cache"]
}
```

Now the coordinator can choose: retry, work around, or ship the partial. The guide is direct: **generic error statuses hide valuable context from the coordinator**.

Two anti-patterns are named explicitly, and they are symmetric:

| Anti-pattern | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| **Silent suppression** — returning an empty result as success | the report says "no competitors found" although the search never ran |
| **Terminating the whole workflow** on a single failure | one subagent's crash kills the entire investigation |

And the same distinction you met in module C7, stated here at the multi-agent level: an **access failure** (a timeout needing a retry decision) is not the same as a **valid empty result** (a successful query with no matches).

Plus the familiar rule: a subagent **recovers from transient failures locally** and propagates only what it could not resolve — with what was attempted and the partial results.

And the objective's final technique: **synthesis output is annotated with coverage** — which findings are well-supported and which topic areas have gaps because sources were unavailable. A report where the holes are invisible is more dangerous than one where they are marked.

## 💻 Practice

```python
# pip install anthropic
import os, json, anthropic

client = anthropic.Anthropic(api_key=os.environ["ANTHROPIC_API_KEY"])
MODEL = "claude-sonnet-5"

# ---------- 5.2: explicit escalation criteria with few-shot ----------
ESCALATION_POLICY = """
Escalate to a human ONLY under one of three conditions:
  1. The customer explicitly asks for a human — escalate immediately, with no investigation first.
  2. Policy does not cover the request or is ambiguous — policy silence does not mean permission.
  3. No meaningful progress is possible: the data is missing, the tools give no answer.

Do NOT escalate because of message tone, and do not treat "confidence" as a complexity signal.
If the customer is frustrated but the issue is within your capability: acknowledge the frustration,
offer a resolution, and escalate only if they reiterate their preference for a human.

If a tool returns MULTIPLE matches — ask for an additional identifier
(order number, email, last digits of the card). Never select by heuristic.

Examples:

Customer: "Put me through to an agent."
→ escalate(reason="explicit_request"). No clarifying questions.

Customer: "Your delivery again! The item arrived broken, order 88214."
→ Do not escalate. Acknowledge: "I understand, that is frustrating" — and arrange the
   replacement: the issue is simple and within capability. Escalate if they ask for a human.

Customer: "A competitor is 15% cheaper, match the price."
→ escalate(reason="policy_gap"). Policy covers price adjustments on our own site only
   and is silent on competitors. Silence is not permission.

The tool returned three customers named Petrova.
→ Do not choose. Ask for the order number or email: "Could you confirm the order number —
   I have several matches on that surname."
"""

# ---------- 5.3: structured error context ----------
def subagent_error(failure_type: str, attempted: str,
                   partial: list | None = None, alternatives: list | None = None) -> dict:
    """An error the coordinator can make a decision from."""
    return {"ok": False, "failure_type": failure_type, "attempted": attempted,
            "partial_results": partial or [], "alternatives": alternatives or []}

def empty_result(query: str) -> dict:
    """A valid empty result is a SUCCESS, not an error."""
    return {"ok": True, "results": [], "query": query, "note": "query ran, no matches"}

def web_subagent(query: str, simulate: str = "ok") -> dict:
    if simulate == "timeout":
        # local recovery was already attempted — we propagate what we could not resolve
        return subagent_error(
            failure_type="timeout",
            attempted=f"search '{query}', 2 attempts at 30s, both timed out",
            partial=[{"claim": "competitor A subscription from $19", "source_url": "https://a.example/pricing"}],
            alternatives=["search each competitor separately",
                          "use last week's cache (data is 7 days older)"])
    if simulate == "empty":
        return empty_result(query)
    return {"ok": True, "results": [{"claim": "…", "source_url": "…"}]}

def coordinator(query: str, simulate: str) -> dict:
    r = web_subagent(query, simulate)

    if r["ok"] and not r["results"]:
        # NOT an error: report it as such, inventing no causes
        return {"status": "completed", "coverage": [{"topic": query, "support": "no_matches"}]}

    if not r["ok"]:
        # The coordinator decides from the error structure — instead of aborting everything
        decision = ("retry_narrow" if "separately" in " ".join(r["alternatives"])
                    else "use_partial")
        return {"status": "degraded", "decision": decision,
                "partial_used": r["partial_results"],
                # coverage annotation: weak areas are visible in the report
                "coverage": [{"topic": query, "support": "partial",
                              "gap_reason": f"{r['failure_type']}: {r['attempted']}"}]}

    return {"status": "completed", "coverage": [{"topic": query, "support": "well_supported"}]}

def support_agent(message: str) -> str:
    resp = client.messages.create(model=MODEL, max_tokens=800, system=ESCALATION_POLICY,
                                  messages=[{"role": "user", "content": message}])
    return "".join(b.text for b in resp.content if b.type == "text")

if __name__ == "__main__":
    for m in ["Put me through to an agent.",
              "Your delivery again! The item from order 88214 arrived broken.",
              "A competitor is 15% cheaper, match the price.",
              "Cancel my order. My name is Petrova."]:
        print(f"\n>>> {m}\n{support_agent(m)[:280]}")

    print("\n--- coordinator ---")
    for sim in ("ok", "empty", "timeout"):
        print(sim, "→", json.dumps(coordinator("competitor pricing", sim))[:220])
```

**Assignment.**

1. Remove the tone paragraph from the policy and feed an irritated message with a simple question. Measure the rate of unnecessary escalations over ten runs.
2. Replace the explicit demand for a human with an implicit one ("you are being useless") and confirm the agent does not escalate automatically — but does escalate when the customer repeats the request directly.
3. Replace the structured error with `{"error": "search unavailable"}` and see what the coordinator can decide. Then restore the structure and compare.
4. Make `web_subagent` return an empty result **as an error** and trace how that turns into a false "no competitors found" conclusion in the report.

## 🎯 On the exam

- **Objective 5.2**: the three appropriate triggers, honouring an explicit request immediately, acknowledging frustration on a simple issue, the unreliability of sentiment and self-reported confidence, escalating on a policy gap, requesting additional identifiers on multiple matches, explicit criteria with few-shot in the system prompt.
- **Objective 5.3**: structured error context (type, attempted, partial results, alternatives), generic statuses hiding context, the silent-suppression and workflow-termination anti-patterns, access failures vs valid empty results, local recovery, coverage annotations in synthesis.
- Scenarios: **Customer Support Resolution Agent** (all of 5.2), **Multi-Agent Research System** (5.3).
- 5.2 trap: "escalate on negative sentiment" or "escalate when model confidence is below a threshold". Both are named unreliable proxies for actual complexity.
- Second 5.2 trap: "attempt a resolution first, then escalate" in response to an **explicit** request for a human. An explicit demand is honoured immediately.
- Third 5.2 trap: picking one of several customer matches by heuristic. The correct move is asking for an additional identifier.
- 5.3 trap: "return an empty result on failure so the workflow does not break". That is silent suppression — an explicitly named anti-pattern, symmetric with terminating the workflow.

## 📌 Remember

- Escalate for three reasons: an explicit customer request, a policy gap or ambiguity, an inability to progress.
- Sentiment and self-reported confidence do not reflect case complexity and must not be triggers.
- An explicit request for a human is honoured immediately. Frustration on a simple issue calls for acknowledging the emotion and resolving it, not occupying an operator.
- Policy silence is not permission. It is a reason to escalate.
- Multiple matches in a tool result call for a clarifying identifier, not a heuristic choice.
- A subagent error carries the failure type, what was attempted, partial results and alternatives. A generic status is useless.
- Silently suppressing an error and terminating the whole workflow are two symmetric anti-patterns.
- Synthesis is annotated with coverage: which findings are supported and where a gap exists because a source was unavailable.
