# Module A19. Unit economics and the CFO memo

> **Level:** Senior AI · **Time:** ~55 minutes · **Format:** situation → theory → practice → quiz

## 🏢 The situation

Annual budget season. Victor, the CFO, calls you in first — earlier than engineers usually get called:

> "I have three decisions. One: do we renew this at all. Two: if we do, hire another manager or expand the agent. Three: a competitor sells something similar for $2000 a month — maybe we should buy instead of build?
>
> 'The agent saves time' will not do. I need money, not impressions."

You realize you already have everything you need: traces with the cost of every run (A14), quality metrics (A6), edit statistics (A17). The only missing piece was translating them into the language Victor speaks.

## 🎯 Your task

1. Compute the honest cost per unit of work — including what people usually forget.
2. Quantify the value in a way a finance person will accept.
3. Answer build vs buy with numbers rather than convictions.

---

## 📚 Theory

### Total cost: what gets forgotten

The provider's invoice is less than half the spend. The full cost per unit of work:

| Component | Example per month | Forgotten? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Model tokens | $410 | No |
| Infrastructure (queues, storage, traces) | $120 | Often |
| Engineer time on support | 20 h × rate | **Almost always** |
| Human time reviewing emails | 45 min/day per manager | **Almost always** |
| Incident and complaint handling | 3 h per month | Always |
| Amortized build cost | 3 months of work / 24 | Always |

The rule: **if a human does it, it is part of the cost**. Reviewing emails is not free just because no invoice arrives for it.

### Three traps in the calculation

1. **Counting saved time directly as money.** Igor is not leaving and his salary does not shrink; he spends the time on something else. The correct phrasing: "we freed 64 hours a month that went into working meetings" — and Victor converts that into money.
2. **Ignoring review time.** If a human spends 45 minutes a day, the automation saves 64 hours, not 80. That difference is a substantial part of the argument.
3. **Forgetting risk.** One public email with an invented fact can cost more than a year of savings. Risk goes into the memo as a line: "worst case and what holds it back" — that raises credibility, it does not lower it.

### Build vs buy

Victor's question about the $2000 competitor is not about price, it is about **what exactly you are buying**:

| Criterion | Favours buy | Favours build |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Data | A common task on common data | Quality depends on your data and context |
| Speed | Needed tomorrow | You have 2–3 months |
| Differentiation | It does not set you apart | It is part of your advantage |
| Integrations | Standard systems | Your own CRM and processes |
| Cost of ownership | One payment, their support | Your engineers forever |

The honest answer is often hybrid: **buy the platform, build what depends on your data**. And back that with numbers too: your solution's cost over a year against the subscription plus migration.

## 💻 Practice

```python
import json, datetime as dt

# --- Inputs: filled in with your finance person, not invented ---
INPUTS = {
    "emails_per_month": 400,
    "minutes_saved_per_email": 12,      # what an SDR spent per email by hand
    "review_minutes_per_day": 45,       # human time reviewing the queue
    "workdays_per_month": 22,
    "sdr_hourly_rate": 25,              # fully loaded rate
    "engineer_hourly_rate": 60,
    "engineer_hours_per_month": 20,     # support and investigation
    "infra_usd_per_month": 120,
    "build_cost_usd": 42000,            # 3 months of work: the build
    "amortization_months": 24,
    "vendor_usd_per_month": 2000,       # the "buy" alternative
    "vendor_migration_usd": 8000,
}

def unit_economics(spend_from_traces, inputs=INPUTS):
    """spend_from_traces: actual model spend from A12/A14."""
    i = inputs
    hours_saved_gross = i["emails_per_month"] * i["minutes_saved_per_email"] / 60
    hours_review = i["review_minutes_per_day"] * i["workdays_per_month"] / 60
    hours_saved_net = hours_saved_gross - hours_review

    cost = {
        "models": round(spend_from_traces, 2),
        "infrastructure": i["infra_usd_per_month"],
        "engineer_support": i["engineer_hours_per_month"] * i["engineer_hourly_rate"],
        "amortized_build": round(i["build_cost_usd"] / i["amortization_months"], 2),
    }
    total_cost = sum(cost.values())
    value_hours_usd = hours_saved_net * i["sdr_hourly_rate"]

    return {
        "cost_per_email": round(total_cost / i["emails_per_month"], 3),
        "total_cost": round(total_cost, 2),
        "cost_breakdown": cost,
        "hours_freed_gross": round(hours_saved_gross, 1),
        "hours_spent_reviewing": round(hours_review, 1),
        "hours_freed_net": round(hours_saved_net, 1),
        "hours_value_usd": round(value_hours_usd, 2),
        "payback_ratio": round(value_hours_usd / total_cost, 2),   # >1 pays for itself on hours
    }


def build_vs_buy(econ, inputs=INPUTS, horizon_months=12):
    """Compare over a horizon, not at a point: the cost structures differ."""
    i = inputs
    run_rate = econ["total_cost"] - econ["cost_breakdown"]["amortized_build"]
    build_total = econ["cost_breakdown"]["amortized_build"] * horizon_months + \
                  run_rate * horizon_months
    buy_total = i["vendor_usd_per_month"] * horizon_months + i["vendor_migration_usd"]
    return {
        "build_one_year": round(build_total, 2),
        "buy_one_year": round(buy_total, 2),
        "difference": round(buy_total - build_total, 2),
        "breakeven_months": round(i["vendor_migration_usd"] /
                                  max(run_rate - i["vendor_usd_per_month"], 0.01), 1)
            if run_rate > i["vendor_usd_per_month"] else None,
    }


def cfo_memo(econ, bvb, quality, risk):
    """One page: numbers, assumptions, risk, the decision being asked for."""
    return f"""
MEMO FOR THE CFO · outbound email agent · {dt.date.today()}

WHAT WE GET
· {econ['hours_freed_net']} h/month of sales time freed
  (gross saving {econ['hours_freed_gross']} h minus {econ['hours_spent_reviewing']} h of review).
· Outbound volume rose from 120 to {INPUTS['emails_per_month']} emails a month with the same headcount.
· Quality: {quality['unedited_rate']:.0%} of emails ship without edits, {quality['complaints']} complaints this quarter.

WHAT IT COSTS
· ${econ['total_cost']}/month in total, of which models ${econ['cost_breakdown']['models']},
  engineer support ${econ['cost_breakdown']['engineer_support']}.
· Cost per email: ${econ['cost_per_email']}.
· Ratio of freed-hours value to spend: {econ['payback_ratio']}×.

BUILD OR BUY (12-month horizon)
· Keep building: ${bvb['build_one_year']}
· Move to the vendor: ${bvb['buy_one_year']} (including ${INPUTS['vendor_migration_usd']} migration)
· Our solution relies on our CRM and knowledge base; the vendor supports that only partially.

RISK
· Worst case: {risk['worst_case']}.
· What holds it back: {risk['controls']}.
· We assess residual risk as {risk['residual']}.

ASSUMPTIONS (check these first)
· SDR rate ${INPUTS['sdr_hourly_rate']}/h fully loaded.
· 12 minutes per manual email — measured over 20 emails in March.
· Freed hours go into working meetings rather than leaving the payroll.

DECISION REQUESTED
Do we renew for a year as is? Expanding to inbound is a separate discussion —
the value case there is different and needs measurement.
""".strip()
```

How to use this in the conversation with Victor:

1. **Open with the cost structure, not the benefits.** A finance person who sees that you counted your own time honestly listens differently to everything after.
2. **Hours and revenue in separate lines.** Hours you measured, revenue you can only estimate: never blend the reliable with the hypothetical.
3. **The risk section raises credibility.** "Worst case and what holds it back" is what they will ask anyway; better to bring it yourself.
4. **Assumptions are written where they can be seen.** Victor corrects the rate, the numbers recompute, and the conversation survives.

**Assignment.** Fill `INPUTS` with your team's real numbers and run `unit_economics` on last month's trace data. If the payback ratio is below 1, that is not a reason to hide the calculation but a reason to discuss which parameter to change: volume, cost per run, or review time.

---

## 📌 Remember

- Total cost includes people's time: engineer support and human review. The provider invoice is under half.
- Saved hours and projected revenue go in separate lines: they carry different reliability.
- Review time is subtracted from the saving. Without it the case is inflated and collapses on the first question.
- Include risk yourself: "worst case and what holds it back" raises trust in the rest of the numbers.
- Build vs buy is decided over a year and on whether quality depends on your data.
- Explicit assumptions keep the model alive: they can be corrected instead of discarded wholesale.
